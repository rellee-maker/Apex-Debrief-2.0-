User guide

Apex Debrief user guide

Apex Debrief turns an AiM data logger session into a driver debrief: where the time is lost, how the driver uses the car, and whether the car is healthy. It runs in your web browser, with nothing to install and no account.

Getting started

What you need

  • A session exported from Race Studio 3 as a CSV file (required).
  • The track's split file from Race Studio 3, an .amss file (optional, but needed for sector times, sector coaching and corner labels).

Export a CSV from Race Studio 3

  1. Open the session in Race Studio 3 Analysis.
  2. Select the session, choose Export, and pick CSV.
  3. Save the file somewhere easy to find.

Raw logger files (.xrk, .drk) can't be read in a browser. If you drop one in, Apex Debrief tells you to export a CSV instead.

Load your data

  1. Click Upload Telemetry, or drag the CSV onto the page.
  2. Click Track Splits and choose the .amss file for that track. You can do this before or after loading the CSV.
  3. The analysis appears straight away. Load several CSVs at once to compare sessions, days or drivers.

Want to look around first? The live demo opens with a real SCCA Spec Racer Ford session from WeatherTech Raceway Laguna Seca already loaded.

Laps & Sectors

Start here: it answers "how fast, how consistent, and where is the time?"

  • Headline numbers: best lap, theoretical best (the best time in every sector added together), average flying lap with its spread (σ), lap count, top speed and peak cornering G.
  • Lap timing table: every lap with its type, gap to the best and sector times. A purple sector is the fastest of the session. Click any lap to compare it with the best lap in Lap Overlay.
  • Flying-lap pace: a bar per flying lap, with the best in green.
  • Sector opportunity: how much time the average flying lap loses in each sector compared with the driver's own best version of it. The longest red bars are where to focus.
  • Session highlights: a short written summary of the session.

Lap Overlay & Delta

Lay two laps on top of each other to see exactly where one gains or loses time.

  • Choose a Reference lap and a Compare lap at the top. Laps are lined up by distance around the track, not by time, so the same corner sits at the same spot on every chart.
  • Delta time builds up through the lap: above zero means the compare lap is behind. A rising line shows where time is being lost.
  • The other charts show speed, throttle and brake (brake as a percentage of the session's hardest stop), RPM and gear, and steering with cornering G.
  • Hover any chart or drag the slider to move a cursor through every chart at once. The gauges and the track map follow it.
  • The track map is coloured red where the compare lap is losing time and green where it is gaining.
  • Sector deltas lists the two laps' sector times side by side.
  • Channel explorer plots any channel in the file, for the two laps or across the whole session.

Coaching & Map

The coaching cards turn the data into specific advice, using only the uploaded session.

  • The map shows the best lap coloured by speed, with the split gates marked.
  • Each sector card compares the driver's fastest version of that sector with their typical one: entry speed, braking point, minimum speed, exit speed, time at full throttle and time coasting. It then says what to change, with a confidence level.
  • Cards are ranked by how much time each sector costs per lap. Show in overlay jumps to that sector in Lap Overlay with the right laps selected.
  • Further cards cover lap-to-lap consistency and throttle commitment.

Driver Inputs

How the driver uses the pedals and the tyres, on flying laps.

  • Pedal summary: time at full throttle, average throttle, coasting (off both pedals), overlap (throttle and brake together, which includes heel-and-toe blips) and brake zones per lap.
  • Traction circle: every dot is one moment on track. Down is braking, up is accelerating, left and right are cornering. The farther from the centre, the harder the tyres are working. The dashed line is the most grip the car showed all session.
  • Dots are coloured by what the driver is doing: braking in a straight line, trail braking, mid-corner, on throttle or coasting. Use Show to view the best lap, one lap, or all flying laps.
  • Trail braking means easing off the brake while turning in, so braking grip hands over to cornering grip without a gap. It fills the bottom corners of the circle. Empty bottom corners mean the driver finishes braking and then turns, leaving grip unused.
  • Brake zone table: every brake zone with its entry and minimum speed, peak deceleration, trail-braking time on the chosen lap against the typical lap, cornering G when the brake comes off, and hand-over G (the lowest grip used during the switch from braking to turning). Each zone is labelled Trail braking, Light trail or Brake, then turn. Click a row to see that zone's brake and cornering traces, with the overlap shaded.
  • Histograms and lap table: how long the driver spends at each throttle and brake level, lap by lap.

Brake is measured as a percentage of the session's hardest stop, so this tab works even if the brake sensor's scaling is wrong.

Shifting

  1. Enter the engine's shift target, rev limit and the RPM where the power band starts. Until you do, defaults are based on the session's peak RPM. Your numbers are remembered for that car in this browser.
  2. Read the results: upshifts per lap, average upshift RPM, early upshifts, downshifts that over-rev, time at full throttle below the power band, and time on the limiter.
  3. The chart places every shift around the lap, so you can see which corners cause trouble. Tables break it down by gear change and by lap, and the findings list says what to work on.

Session Comparison

Load two or more CSVs (practice and qualifying, day 1 and day 2, or two drivers in the same car) and compare them here.

  • The sessions table lets you choose which sessions to show, which is the reference (Ref), and which lap to compare from each. It lists each session's best, theoretical best, average, consistency, top speed, air and track temperature, and peak oil and coolant temperature.
  • Charts overlay the chosen laps' speed and show the time gained or lost against the reference.
  • The sector table shows each session's best time per sector, the fastest in purple, and the theoretical best across all sessions combined.
  • A line chart shows flying-lap times through each session, and a short summary says where each session gains or loses time.
  • Sessions from a different track are detected by GPS and left out of the overlay.
  • Conditions shows the weather at the track at session time: air temperature, an estimated track temperature, humidity, wind (including headwind or tailwind on the main straight) and density altitude, which affects engine power.

Racing Line

Compare the line two laps took, corner by corner, and what it cost or gained.

  • Choose a reference lap (blue) and a compare lap (red). Dots mark each lap's slowest point in every corner.
  • Scroll on the map to zoom, drag to pan and double-click to reset. Zoom jumps to a corner. Exaggerate draws the gap between the lines larger than reality so small differences are visible; the map labels when it is not to true scale.
  • The corner table says whether the compare lap's line at the apex was tighter or wider, the biggest difference in the corner, the minimum speed of each lap, and the time gained or lost. Click a row to zoom to that corner.
  • Below the map, one chart shows how far apart the lines are along the lap, and another shows both laps' speed at the same point on track.

Corners are found where the car slows down, so flat-out kinks are not listed. GPS is accurate to a few feet; the tab shows the accuracy for your file, and differences smaller than that are noise.

Channel Audit

Every channel in the file, checked so you know which numbers to trust.

  • Each row shows the unit in the file, the unit shown, minimum, average and maximum, and what the channel is used for.
  • OK means the channel looks right. Note means it was corrected automatically, for example a voltage logged 1,000× too large. Check scaling means the values are implausible, such as oil pressure of 4 psi at 6,700 rpm. No signal means the channel never changed.
  • To correct a mis-calibrated sensor, type a multiplier in Scale. Every tab updates, and the correction is remembered for that car in this browser. Compare against a mechanical gauge to find the right number.

PDF debrief and clean CSV

  • PDF Debrief builds a two-page report: headline numbers, the best-lap map, all lap times, the top coaching points, shifting, engine flags and the brake-zone table. In the print window, choose Save as PDF.
  • Export Clean CSV downloads the session in US units with any scale corrections applied, plus lap number and lap distance on every row.

Units, privacy and troubleshooting

Units. Everything is shown in US units: mph, °F, psi, feet and inches. Files logged in metric are converted automatically, including files saved on Windows where the degree symbol is garbled.

Privacy. Your files are processed in your browser and never uploaded. The only thing sent anywhere is the track's location and the session date, to the free Open-Meteo weather service, to look up conditions. Scale corrections and shift settings are saved in this browser only.
